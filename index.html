Foreign nationals and non-resident international buyers are legally entitled to purchase real estate with 100% freehold ownership rights in designated investment zones established by the Government of Dubai. In freehold areas, buyers receive absolute, unrestricted title deeds issued by the Dubai Land Department (DLD), permitting ownership, leasing, resale, and inheritance without local partnership requirements. Foreign ownership outside designated freehold areas is typically subject to leasehold or long-term usufruct arrangements.

UAE real estate offers a compelling combination of advantages for international investors, including 0% personal income and capital gains taxes on residential property, a currency pegged to the US Dollar providing macroeconomic stability, robust population and economic growth, high infrastructure quality, and transparent regulatory governance under authorities such as RERA. Additionally, qualifying property investments offer residency pathways through the UAE Golden Visa programme. Market returns, rental yields, and capital appreciation vary across individual developments and market conditions.

Under current UAE immigration regulations, property investors can qualify for the 10-Year Renewable Golden Visa by acquiring one or more residential properties with a combined valuation of AED 2,000,000 or greater. The qualifying threshold applies to both completed and approved off-plan properties from registered developers. The Golden Visa provides long-term UAE residency for the principal investor, their spouse, children, and domestic staff, with no minimum stay requirements. Final eligibility and residency issuance remain subject to official immigration approval and regulatory compliance.

The statutory minimum qualifying investment for the UAE 10-Year Real Estate Golden Visa is currently AED 2,000,000 (approximately USD 545,000). The property value must be reflected on the official Dubai Land Department title deed or pre-title registration (Oqood). Both equity-funded purchases and properties acquired with UAE bank mortgages are eligible, provided the financial criteria established by immigration authorities are fulfilled at the time of application.

Yes, non-residents of all nationalities may purchase freehold property in designated zones across the UAE without needing a UAE residence visa or physical presence for every stage of transaction. Purchases can be initiated and concluded through secure digital execution, international bank transfer, or appointed legal power of attorney (POA). A valid passport is the primary requirement to reserve property and begin title conveyancing.

In addition to the base purchase price, buyers should account for standard acquisition fees. In Dubai, these typically include the Dubai Land Department (DLD) transfer fee of 4% plus administrative charges, property registration trustee fees (AED 2,000 to AED 4,000 depending on valuation), and Oqood pre-registration fees for off-plan units. When utilizing mortgage financing, bank arrangement, valuation, and mortgage registration fees apply. Conveyancing, legal advisory, and developer administration fees may also vary by transaction.

Yes, off-plan properties are widely available across SOBHA developments. Off-plan acquisitions are structured with construction-linked payment plans, allowing purchasers to pay in scheduled instalments spread across the construction timeline—such as an initial booking deposit followed by staged payments tied to verified build milestones, with the remaining balance due upon completion and handover. Specific payment schedules, milestone percentages, and completion dates vary by individual project and release phase.

Off-plan buyer funds in Dubai are strictly regulated by the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) under Law No. 8 of 2007. Developers must register each project, hold accredited title to the land, and establish a dedicated project Escrow Account with an authorized financial institution. All buyer instalments are deposited directly into this protected account, and funds can only be released to the developer in phases upon independent engineering certification of construction progress, safeguarding investor capital throughout development.

Yes, property owners in the UAE are fully entitled to lease their residences to generate rental income through either long-term residential tenancies governed by the standard Ejari tenancy framework or short-term holiday home letting under Department of Economy and Tourism (DET) licensing. Dubai has historically maintained attractive rental yields compared to global gateway capitals. However, net yields depend on prime location, unit layout, seasonal occupancy, market demand, and ongoing property management fees.

Ongoing ownership costs primarily consist of annual service charges and maintenance fees, which cover common area upkeep, building security, concierge, swimming pool and fitness amenities, landscaping, and building reserve funds. In Dubai, service charges are calculated on a per-square-foot basis and must be formally approved by RERA's Mollak system, ensuring full transparency. Owners should also factor in local utility connections (DEWA), cooling services (district cooling if applicable), and comprehensive property insurance.
